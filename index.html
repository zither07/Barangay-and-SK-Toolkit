    Foundations

    Official communication is any message that meets three tests. A Facebook message can be official only if it meets all three.

    Authorized
    Sent by someone with the right to speak for the office.
    Purpose-driven
    It asks, informs, certifies or acts.
    Documented
    It can be filed, traced and audited.

    Why it matters in the barangay and SK

    • It carries government authority. Every letter reflects the integrity of the LGU or SK.
    • It becomes a record. Letters end up in public files, COA audits and legal references.
    • It prevents confusion. One unclear instruction can delay a project for a whole purok.
    • It builds public trust. Residents judge the office by how well it writes.

    Official and casual letters compared

    FeatureOfficial letterCasual letter
    PurposeInstitutional; documented and traceablePersonal; no record needed
    StructureFixed: letterhead, date, inside address, subject, salutation, body, closing, signatureFlexible: greeting, body, sign-off
    ToneFormal, objective, respectfulFriendly, conversational
    LanguageStandard grammar, full sentences, no slangEveryday language, idioms, slang
    LengthConcise but complete, with action itemsVaries

    Qualities and principles

    Effective official communication has seven qualities and follows four principles.

    Seven qualities

    QualityWhat it looks like in practice
    AccuracyCorrect names, titles, dates, amounts and references
    ClarityOne purpose per letter, stated in the first paragraph
    BrevityNo flowery openings; every sentence does work
    FormalityNo “po, hehe”, emojis or text-speak
    ObjectivityFacts and requested outcomes, not feelings
    ConsistencySame format, letterhead and spelling across all office letters
    CompletenessWho, what, when, where, what action, what is attached

    Four guiding principles

    • Purpose first. The reader should know what you want by the end of paragraph 1.
    • Reader-centred. Write for the person acting on it: what do they need to decide or do?
    • Record-ready. Assume the letter will be filed, audited or read aloud in a session.
    • Professional tone. Respectful without begging; firm without sounding harsh.

    Formats and forms of address

    Use full block format for almost everything: every line starts at the left margin, with no indentions, in Arial or Times New Roman, size 12, with 1-inch margins.

    FormatLayoutUse it for
    Full blockAll lines at the left margin; no indentionsNearly all LGU letters; the standard
    Modified blockDate, closing and signature aligned right; body leftAcceptable alternative; never mix with full block
    SimplifiedNo salutation; SUBJECT LINE IN ALL CAPSPublic notices and announcements

    Each line below was found in a real workshop draft. Decide on the fix before you reveal it.

    The common mistakes

    • Missing or incorrect dates
    • Misspelled names, wrong office names, “Hon.” used for appointed officials
    • Long introductions and unnecessary greetings
    • Emotional or subjective phrasing
    • Missing specifics: what, when, where, how many, how much
    • Group-chat announcements with no written record
    • Informal tone (“Good day po, hehe, thank you so much po in advance”)
    • Attachments mentioned but not enclosed
    • Mixed letter formats in one letter

    Speaking officially

    Secretaries speak too: reading notices, presenting minutes, reporting at assemblies and hosting programs. For barangay and SK officials, speaking is service.

    Build every talk in four steps

    1. HookAn opening that fits the audience: a question, a local fact, a short story.
    2. MessageOne main point, said plainly.
    3. EvidenceNumbers, dates and examples from your barangay.
    4. ActionWhat you want people to do, and by when.

    Choose your delivery

    MethodWhen a secretary uses it
    Manuscript (reading)Minutes, resolutions, official notices, where accuracy matters more than eye contact
    MemorizedShort opening lines or a program’s welcome
    Extemporaneous (from an outline)Assembly reports, briefings, coordination meetings
    ImpromptuAnswering questions from the floor

    Why take the mic

    PurposeWhat it sounds like
    InformShare agenda items, schedules and requirements clearly
    PersuadeBuild support for a youth or barangay initiative
    InspireMove residents or peers to act
    Build communityStrengthen connection and trust

    Before you speak

    Practice line: say “Our youth deserve meaningful opportunities” twice, first rushed, then with a steady voice and a pause before “meaningful.” Same words, different impact.
